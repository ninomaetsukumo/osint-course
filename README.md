## Learning resources and acknowledgments

The project creator has collected reference books, public methodology guides and research resources to guide course development. **The sources below are not endorsements, sponsors, affiliations, or proof of certification.** Ninomae OSINT lessons are original educational adaptations and practical exercises, not copies of the source publications. Students should consult originals when possible.

### References explicitly cited in the five committed Foundations lesson files

- Rae Baker — *Deep Dive: Exploring the Real-world Value of Open Source Intelligence*. Cited for introductory OSINT methods, the intelligence cycle, searching and basic OPSEC.
- Richards J. Heuer Jr. — *Psychology of Intelligence Analysis*. Cited for analytical assumptions, cognitive biases, competing hypotheses and uncertainty.
- Wayne C. Booth, Gregory G. Colomb and Joseph M. Williams — *The Craft of Research*. Cited for research questions, arguments, source handling and evidence.
- United Nations OHCHR and UC Berkeley Human Rights Center — *Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source Investigations* (2022). Cited throughout the Foundations modules for investigation process, verification, documentation, privacy and security. [Official publication](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/2024-01/OHCHR_BerkeleyProtocol.pdf).

The lesson files provide module-specific reading notes. This list is based on the references **actually present in the five committed `lessons.md` files** as reviewed on 13 October 2026.
