## F05.01 — Legal and Ethical Boundaries of OSINT

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes

### Learning objectives
Explain why publicly accessible information still requires legal and ethical judgment, recognize unauthorized access, and identify when to seek guidance.

### Lawful OSINT
OSINT commonly uses relevant public or otherwise authorized information such as official publications, accessible registers and company announcements. Public availability does not mean unlimited rights to collect, reuse, disclose or retain information.

### Public versus unauthorized
Reading a published municipal decision is ordinary public-source research. Using another person's employee account credentials without appropriate authorization is not. Client interest alone is not permission.

### Legal and ethical are not identical
Technical accessibility does not settle whether collection or disclosure is appropriate. Consider applicable law, purpose, necessity, proportionality, privacy and possible harm.

### Beginner checklist
- Is the information relevant to the approved question?
- Is access and use authorized?
- Could collection or disclosure unnecessarily harm someone?
- Is specialist advice needed?

### Guided activity
Research a fictional school's authorization using an official register. Decline an offer of unauthorized employee database access.

### Lesson check — five questions (pass 4/5)
1. Does publicly accessible mean unrestricted use for every purpose?
2. Is client interest enough to enter a private account?
3. Can an official public register be relevant to lawful OSINT?
4. Should potential harm affect collection decisions?
5. Should uncertain authorization questions sometimes be escalated?

## F05.02 — Privacy and Data Minimization

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes

### Learning objectives
Understand personal information, data minimization, necessary versus irrelevant collection, and sensitive-information handling.

### Personal information
Personal information can include names, home addresses, private contact details and other identifiers associated with identifiable people. Sensitivity depends on context and applicable rules.

### Data minimization
Collect and process only information justified by a legitimate specific purpose, necessary to achieve it, and proportionate to that purpose. The *Berkeley Protocol* addresses data minimization in Section II.

### Example
To establish municipal approval, an official authorization decision and its date are relevant. An unrelated spreadsheet containing employees' home addresses and relatives' phone numbers is not.

### Avoid over-collection
Irrelevant data creates privacy, storage, security and review risks.

### If unnecessary sensitive information appears
Do not spread it or include it in findings. If already received, restrict handling and follow applicable policies about retention, deletion, notification or preservation. Do not assume immediate destruction is always lawful.

### Guided activity
Choose a fictional corporate approval record rather than an unrelated personal-address spreadsheet when answering an approval question.

### Lesson check — five questions (pass 4/5)
1. Does minimization reduce unnecessary collection?
2. Is an unrelated home address automatically relevant to public corporate approval?
3. Can over-collection increase privacy risks?
4. Should sensitive information enter a report merely because it appears online?
5. Can preservation duties affect disposal?

## F05.03 — Investigator Safety and Basic OPSEC

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes

### Learning objectives
Define operational security, recognize common risks, identify basic precautions and know when expert help is required.

### What is OPSEC?
OPSEC means **Operational Security**: identifying and managing risks from investigations to researchers, other people, methods and sensitive information. It does not permit unlawful activity.

### Possible risks
| Category | Example |
|---|---|
| Digital | Malicious file or link |
| Account | Compromised password |
| Privacy | Exposed sensitive notes |
| Physical | Unwanted real-world safety threat |
| Psychological | Distressing-content exposure |

### Beginner precautions
- Update software and devices.
- Use unique passwords and multifactor authentication where available.
- Treat unexpected files and links cautiously.
- Separate personal and professional work where feasible.
- Limit access to research files.
- Avoid unnecessary direct engagement with research subjects.

### Risk assessment
Ask what could go wrong, who could be affected, which precautions are available and whether the work is appropriate to your authority and skill level.

### Escalate
Significant safety threats, vulnerable individuals and specialized digital risks require appropriate specialist guidance.

### Guided activity
A suspicious site offers an irrelevant private-data download. Do not download it; document why you excluded it if relevant.

### Lesson check — five questions (pass 4/5)
1. What does OPSEC stand for?
2. Can online research create security risks?
3. Are updates and strong authentication reasonable precautions?
4. Should professional and personal work be separated where practical?
5. Can high-risk work require specialist assistance?

## F05.04 — Responsible Evidence Handling and Preservation

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes

### Learning objectives
Understand preservation, source context, original versus working copies, and justified retention.

### Why preserve evidence?
Relevant online material can change or disappear. Appropriate authorized preservation supports traceability.

### Preserve context
A screenshot alone may not establish source, date, completeness or authenticity. Where lawful and appropriate, record:
- Evidence ID
- Original issuer and location
- Publication, event and access dates as applicable
- Relevant content and how collected
- Known limitations
- Safeguards, access and storage

### Originals and working copies
Distinguish retained source material from personal notes, annotations and edited versions. Never present modified material as unmodified original evidence.

### Storage and retention
Limit access and follow applicable legal, case and organizational retention and deletion requirements. Preservation obligations may sometimes require continued retention.

### Guided activity
Create an evidence-log entry for a fictional official approval, including issuer, title, decision date, access date and limitation.

### Lesson check — five questions (pass 4/5)
1. Can online evidence disappear or change?
2. Does a screenshot alone always prove authenticity?
3. Should original records be distinguished from working notes?
4. Should source context and dates be recorded?
5. Should retention follow applicable obligations?

## F05.05 — Reporting, Stopping and Escalation

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes

### Learning objectives
Choose a responsible stopping decision, explain research limits and identify justified follow-up or specialist escalation.

### Why stopping matters
OSINT does not require discovering every possible detail. Stop when the scoped question has been addressed sufficiently, when further collection lacks justification, or when legal, ethical or safety limits prevent proceeding.

### Three possible decisions
1. **Stop and report:** Give supported findings, uncertainties and limitations.
2. **Continue with authorized targeted research:** Pursue a specifically justified proportionate next step.
3. **Stop and escalate:** Seek appropriate legal, privacy, security or specialist guidance.

### Example
A fictional business receives formal branch approval on 18 September, plans to open on 5 October, and faces an unsupported allegation of secret September operations. An acquaintance offers employee-account credentials.

Decline unauthorized access. Report approval as supported and actual operations by 30 September as unresolved. Propose only warranted, authorized follow-up.

### Reporting responsibly
Use neutral language and separate verified facts, allegations, assumptions and unknowns. Exclude irrelevant personal information.

### Final capstone preparation
The approved Northbridge Expansion Claim capstone evaluates distinctions between approval, planned opening and actual activity, as well as privacy and access decisions.

### Guided activity
Write four sentences covering approval, unresolved operating status, excluded access, and a stopping decision.

### Lesson check — five questions (pass 4/5)
1. Must all unknowns be resolved before stopping?
2. Can unresolved findings be professionally reported?
3. Does a client's desire for certainty justify unauthorized access?
4. Can an authorized targeted follow-up be appropriate?
5. Should researchers escalate risks beyond their competence?
