## F04.01 — What Does Verification Mean?

**Estimated time:** 15 minutes

### Learning objectives
Explain verification, distinguish authenticity from accuracy, and identify source, digital item and content verification.

Verification checks whether information is accurate or valid. A genuine photograph does not automatically prove a caption about its location or date.

### Three aspects of verification
1. **Source:** Who supplied the information? How might they know?
2. **Digital item:** What document, photograph or file is it? What is known about its integrity and origin?
3. **Content:** Does it establish the claimed event, date, place or circumstance?

These are the three considerations described by the *Berkeley Protocol*, Section VI.E.

### Authenticity and accuracy
An authentic announcement establishes what was announced, not necessarily that the announced event occurred. A genuine photograph may have a misleading caption.

### When verification is incomplete
Record what has and has not been verified. Do not claim certainty merely because an item appears convincing.

### Practice
A school announces that a campus will open next month. What can be established? The school made the announcement, but completed opening is not established.

### Lesson check — five questions (passing threshold 4/5)
1. Does verification involve evaluating accuracy or validity?
2. Can an authentic document contain claims that remain unverified?
3. Should the source, digital item and content be considered together?
4. Does a genuine photograph automatically prove its caption?
5. Should unresolved verification questions be recorded?

## F04.02 — Evaluating Source Credibility

**Estimated time:** 15 minutes

### Learning objectives
Evaluate a source's relevance, credibility and limitations; avoid treating anonymity alone as proof of falsehood.

Credibility concerns whether a source has characteristics that make it trustworthy for a particular claim.

Ask: Who produced the information, if known? How would they know? Is it original or repeated? What evidence is provided? Are there reasons to question it?

### Common source types
| Source | Potential value | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Official decision register | Documents a formal decision | May not establish later activity |
| Company announcement | Establishes a statement by the company | May describe a plan |
| News article | Context and reporting | Check underlying sourcing |
| Anonymous firsthand account | Possible relevant lead | Origin and reliability may be hard to establish |
| Repost | Helps trace circulation | May add no new evidence |

### Anonymity
The *Berkeley Protocol* explains that authorship may be unknown, yet authenticity can sometimes be assessed by other means. Neither automatic acceptance nor automatic rejection is justified solely by anonymity.

### Context matters
A corporate registry may establish a registration entry but cannot automatically establish yesterday's customer activity.

### Practice
A news report says a branch began trading but cites only an approval record. Determine which claim remains unsupported.

### Lesson check — five questions (passing threshold 4/5)
1. Can a source be useful while incomplete?
2. Is an official source authoritative about every related fact?
3. Should a researcher examine how a source obtained its information?
4. Does anonymity necessarily prove a claim false?
5. Should copied reporting automatically count as new evidence?

## F04.03 — Independent Corroboration

**Estimated time:** 15 minutes

### Learning objectives
Explain corroboration, identify dependent reporting and distinguish separate evidence from repeated claims.

Corroboration is additional evidence supporting a specific claim. Independence matters.

### Dependent chain
- Article A reports a library opened, citing committee minutes.
- Article B repeats A without new evidence.
- Article C repeats B without new evidence.

This is one reporting chain, not three independent confirmations. Moreover, committee minutes recommending approval do not establish actual opening.

### Relevant independent evidence
An independently issued, authenticated official authorization may establish approval. A separately verified operational record may support actual operation. Evidence for one proposition does not automatically prove the other.

### Independence is not a new website
Different publication names, dates, or formats do not automatically create independent sources. Trace each claim to its origin.

### Practice
A, B and C repeat a company's announcement. D is an independently issued municipal decision. Which record independently supports approval, assuming it is verified? D.

### Lesson check — five questions (passing threshold 4/5)
1. Can several websites depend on the same original report?
2. Does copying a claim create independent evidence?
3. Should independent corroboration address the same claim?
4. Is the number of sources alone enough to establish reliability?
5. Can a separately issued official record provide additional evidence for a formal decision?

## F04.04 — Alternative Explanations and Analytical Bias

**Estimated time:** 15 minutes

### Learning objectives
Recognize confirmation bias, compare plausible hypotheses and avoid unsupported certainty.

Bias can affect which evidence a researcher notices or trusts. **Confirmation bias** means favoring information consistent with an existing belief.

### Alternative explanations
For a fictional branch-opening allegation:
- **H1:** The branch was operating before the deadline.
- **H2:** It was approved but not yet operating.
- **H3:** Its operational status remains unresolved on the supplied evidence.

These are possibilities, not proven facts.

### Test explanations
Ask which records support each hypothesis, which records challenge it, and what remains unknown. Heuer's *Psychology of Intelligence Analysis* advocates examining competing plausible hypotheses; this is a simplified beginner exercise, not his full ACH method.

### Do not overstate absence
No evidence proving operation by a deadline does not necessarily prove that operation did not occur.

### Practice
Write two plausible explanations for a school that announced an opening but has no independently verified attendance records. Name one type of evidence that could distinguish them.

### Lesson check — five questions (passing threshold 4/5)
1. Can expectations influence evidence evaluation?
2. Should researchers examine evidence challenging their preferred explanation?
3. Is an analytical hypothesis a verified fact?
4. Does missing evidence always prove the opposite claim?
5. Can multiple explanations remain plausible after research?

## F04.05 — Evidence Evaluation and Defensible Findings

**Estimated time:** 15 minutes

### Learning objectives
Connect conclusions to evidence, distinguish supported, contradicted and unresolved findings, and communicate limits.

A defensible finding identifies the evidence and explains the reasoning without claiming more than it establishes.

### Three teaching categories
- **Supported:** Relevant evidence establishes the claim within its scope.
- **Contradicted:** Relevant evidence provides grounds against the claim.
- **Unresolved:** The available evidence is insufficient to establish or refute it.

These are simplified learning categories, not a universal formal intelligence confidence scale.

### Write a finding
1. **Finding:** What can be concluded?
2. **Evidence:** Which records support it?
3. **Reasoning:** Why?
4. **Limitations:** What remains uncertain?

### Example
- E001 records official approval on 18 September.
- E002 announces a scheduled opening on 5 October.
- E003 alleges earlier trading without support.

A sound finding: "Formal approval before 30 September is supported by E001. The supplied documents do not establish actual commercial activity by that date. E002 describes a later intended opening and E003 remains an unsupported allegation."

### Practice
Write a four-sentence conclusion that identifies a finding, its evidence, reasoning, and one limitation.

### Lesson check — five questions (passing threshold 4/5)
1. Should conclusions be connected to identifiable evidence?
2. Can a claim remain unresolved after a reasonable investigation?
3. Does an announced future event prove it happened?
4. Should material limitations be included?
5. Can findings be defensible without absolute certainty?
